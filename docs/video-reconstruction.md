# Local video → editable 3D replay

Open **Video replay** in the application, or use `?mode=reconstruct`.
This is an **experimental, assisted reconstruction tool**, not an exact video-to-physics converter or an automatic referee. It runs entirely in the browser on the existing GitHub Pages site. There is no account requirement, video upload, inference service or paid backend.

## Workflow

1. **Open recording** from your computer. The original file is never modified. The initial range now covers the entire recording (up to 60 minutes), not just the first 600 seconds.
2. Choose **Use whole recording**, or set **From second / To second**, then **Add clip**. A half can have any duration; clips are limited to 60 minutes each. Add clips in replay order, omitting pauses if desired. **Trim selected clip to range** removes unwanted portions and their replay events, not source footage. Use a separate clip after camera changes. One project references one source recording.
3. Click the four corners of the **white playing rectangle**, not the outer wall. From the blue-goal end looking down the field, click near left, near right, far right, far left. The numbered diagram and overlay show the order. Calibration maps the image to the simulator's 1.58 × 2.19 m playing rectangle.
4. Seek to a clear frame where all actors are separated and click **Use this frame as reference**. It can be anywhere in the recording: a reference at 6:40 can still reconstruct a clip beginning at 0:00. Identify **Ball, Blue 1, Blue 2, Yellow 1, Yellow 2** there. Team identity is your assignment, not inferred from shirt/robot colours. Adjust each ring to fit the visible body or ball; exclude shadows, wires and projecting loops. Choose 42 mm or the older 74 mm ball and the blue team's attack direction. If the reference has a different camera view, recalibrate its four corners.
5. Choose **Tracking interval**, then **Track clip locally**. The default **100 ms (10 samples/s)** captures fast motion; **500 ms (2 samples/s)** is coarser and **50 ms (20 samples/s)** costs more processing. The interval is recorded per clip; changing it requires retracking. A local worker detects objects across the whole field on every sampled frame. Missing identities remain available for re-detection; loss no longer permanently disables them. Large image changes leave gaps and propose camera-cut markers, but processing continues through the full range and can recover when the calibrated view returns. Different views require separate calibration. Cancel keeps completed samples.
6. Compare the original and reconstructed view. Seek on either timeline, orbit the 3D field, or choose a preset. Low-quality tracks are labelled uncertain; lost actors are omitted. Click **Correct position**, choose an actor and click its visible centre in the source. Correct heading if known, or mark it unseen. **Continue tracking from here** uses visible corrections and current alignment while retaining reference identities for hidden actors. It replaces later samples after confirmation. Detection/recovery counts are activity statistics, **not measured accuracy**.
7. Review suggested events. Only **confirmed** goals or **Set score** entries change the score. Enter the score shown at your first clip manually if the recording begins mid-game. Add goals, out of bounds, damaged, pushed out, kickoff, pauses, periods, side swaps and notes on the timeline. Dismiss an event to reverse its score effect. Retracking preserves reviewed decisions.
8. **Save replay** downloads a `.rcj-replay.json` file. **Load replay** plays it without the original video. Relink the original recording only when you want to check footage or track again. Save explicitly before closing: this workspace has no automatic cloud or browser backup.
9. **Export 3D video** records the full edited timeline with score and confirmed events at 720p or 1080p. Keep the tab visible. Export takes approximately replay duration, has no audio, and uses the browser's supported WebM or MP4 encoder. Leaving the tab or stopping produces a clearly named partial video. Downloads stay local.

## What is and is not recovered

For precise inspection, use the **−500 ms / −100 ms / +100 ms / +500 ms** buttons below the original recording. Source and replay clocks show milliseconds. The slider navigates the whole recording, so a small mouse movement on a long recording can still jump several seconds; the buttons seek by exact time increments instead. Seeking alone does not create new tracking samples.

- A four-point projective calibration handles fixed overhead and oblique cameras. Supported white boundary segments provide bounded, periodic alignment for small camera movements; missing/ambiguous lines keep the last accepted calibration. This is not automatic calibration of arbitrary camera cuts or large pans. For oblique views, the ground-offset control compensates approximately for tracking a robot's top rather than its floor centre. Strong side views, zoom/pan, occlusions, hands and flying balls still require corrections or new clips.
- Whole-field foreground-density proposals are jointly matched to at most four seeded robot identities. Fixed two-ring colour descriptors tolerate rotation; short-term motion helps re-identification. Templates do not automatically learn from their own predictions, avoiding cumulative identity drift. An explicit unmatched option avoids forcing four robots onto clutter when robots are removed. Ball candidates use separate small-object checks. This is a local, seeded detector, **not a trained YOLO model**. Quality scores are heuristic, not calibrated probabilities. Similar robots can still swap; hands, boundary clutter and dark robot hardware remain difficult. Hidden balls are left as gaps, not invented from expected possession.
- Position is estimated on the field plane. Robot heading follows movement unless manually corrected; an omnidirectional robot's actual heading cannot be inferred reliably that way. Ball height and exact spin are not recovered. The 3D actors use the app's chosen robot design and current 2026 field, not reconstructed CAD, historical markings or original textures. Ball diameter follows the clip setting; its visual colour remains the simulator's orange.
- Replays render stored positions directly. They do not run a physics solver that could alter the recorded trajectory, do not recreate robot firmware, and do not enforce nonintersection on uncertain image-derived positions.
- Automatic event suggestions cover possible goal-back-wall contact, possible wall contact, tracking gaps and camera cuts. They are **not rulings**. An out of bounds call is not simply crossing a white line. Pushed-out exceptions, preceding infringements, possession, damage and legal goals require human review. Whole-robot penalty-area entry is not automatically detected in this version.
- Scoreboard OCR, automatic half/pause detection, whistle recognition and full referee-event classification are **not implemented**. An overlay or lack of sound does not prevent visual tracking; overlay scores and period details are entered manually. A side-swap marker is a note; set the following clip's attack direction separately.
- Arbitrary uncalibrated side/cut footage cannot be promised an exact, unattended reconstruction. The current tool is useful for making reviewed teaching replays, not replacing video evidence or certification assessments.

## Files, compatibility and resource bounds

The browser uses a local `File` object URL and decodes frames incrementally at a maximum tracking width of 960 px. It does not read a multi-gigabyte recording into one JavaScript buffer. Processing speed and codec support depend on the computer/browser. H.264 MP4 is the safest starting point; MKV/MOV/WebM work only if the browser can decode their contents. For unsupported recordings, create a separate local H.264 MP4 copy with your video editor or FFmpeg. Audio is optional and never analysed. No remote conversion is performed.

The versioned replay contains source filename/size/duration/resolution, calibrations, seeds, timestamped actor samples, confidence/provenance and reviewed events. It does not contain source video, audio, filesystem paths, accounts or credentials. Anyone you share this file with can read its metadata and your notes. It is not encrypted and is intentionally editable; it is a different format from certification evidence and cannot certify an account.

Imports validate the schema, finite coordinates, chronological samples, clip bounds and unique event IDs. Limits: 200 clips, 100,000 sampled frames across the project, 20,000 events, 128 MB replay file, 512 MB buffered video export. One hour at 20 samples/s fits the replay capacity. Export long projects in shorter clips to avoid the video memory limit. A cancelled tracker keeps completed samples; if no new sample was produced, existing tracking remains intact.

## Verification and implementation

See [validation method, detector choices and measured limitations](reconstruction-validation.md).

- `pnpm test:reconstruction` covers calibration, validation, clip mapping, interpolation gaps, rotation wrapping, corrections, reviewed scoring, trimming, event deduplication, media-seek readiness, synthetic orange/dark ball tracking, camera cuts and hour-long save capacity.
- Optional full-sequence benchmark: `node scripts/benchmark-match-reconstruction.mjs <ffmpeg-path> <Finale_lightweight.mp4> <manual-labels.json> <local-output.json> 10 1725.8 960`. This fixture uses the reference at 400 seconds, processes the full 28:45.8 recording chronologically, and separately reports detection, correct identities, centre error and false positives against manual labels. Sequential FFmpeg decoding is not a browser speed benchmark. Footage, extracted frames and generated tracking outputs remain local; they are not distributed with the site.
- Components are lazy-loaded under `components/reconstruction/`; portable data, geometry, event proposals and worker tracking are under `lib/reconstruction/`. Existing game physics and certification verification remain separate. The shared viewport's post-render callback exports while the WebGL framebuffer is valid; score/event timestamps use the same committed frame time as its poses.

Technical references: [OpenCV's homography explanation](https://docs.opencv.org/4.13.0/d9/dab/tutorial_homography.html), [browser media-type support](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/HTMLMediaElement/canPlayType), [MediaRecorder](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/MediaRecorder), and [encoder capability checks](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/MediaRecorder/isTypeSupported_static).
